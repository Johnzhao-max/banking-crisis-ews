## Decisions

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sample: countries | All 163 countries that appear in WDI, WEO, the Global Debt Database and the Laeven-Valencia country list. No population cut-off. | A size threshold is arbitrary and would remove well-documented cases such as Iceland and Luxembourg in 2008. Thin data is handled by the missing-data rules instead. |
| Sample: countries not in the crisis database | The 34 WEO economies that are not on the Laeven-Valencia country list are excluded. | The list includes countries that never had a crisis (for example Hong Kong SAR), so absence from it means 'not covered', not 'no crisis'. |
| Sample: years | Predictor years 1980 to 2023. | 40 of the 164 crises start in the 1980s and the IMF's model also starts in 1980. The target looks two years ahead and crisis dates end in 2025, so 2023 is the last usable year. |
| Label: crisis dates | Laeven and Valencia (2026), IMF WP 2026/094, Appendix Table A1: 164 episodes. Extracted from the PDF because no data file was published. | Most recent edition. All 151 episodes of the 2020 edition's data file are present with identical start years. |
| Label: start year conflicts | For Angola, Republic of Congo and Ghana the paper's Table 1 and Table A1 give different start years. Table A1 is used; Table 1's year is kept in a separate column. | Table A1 is the complete database table. Only three episodes are affected, by one or two years. |
| Label: borderline episodes | The 13 episodes flagged as borderline count as crises in the baseline. | Matches the total the authors report. A robustness check without them is planned. |
| Label: Yugoslavia | 'Yugoslavia, SFR' is not mapped to any current country. | It dissolved without a single successor and has no banking crisis in the database. |
| Country codes | World Bank ISO3 codes are the standard. Kosovo is XKX (WEO: KOS, DataMapper: UVK); West Bank and Gaza is PSE (IMF: WBG). | The three sources use different codes for these two economies. |
| WEO projections | Years after each series' 'latest actual annual data' are flagged as projections and excluded from the sample. | The October 2026 WEO runs to 2031. Projections are forecasts, not data. |
| WEO fiscal-year countries | For entries such as 'FY2025/26' the first year is taken as the latest actual year. Rows that leave the field blank take the country's most common value. | Conservative: it can treat one observed year as a projection but never the reverse. |
| FRED frequency | Monthly and daily series are converted to annual averages. The incomplete current year is dropped. | The model uses annual data; an average describes the whole year, and a partial-year average is not comparable. |
| Global risk measure | The Baa-Treasury credit spread is used instead of the VIX. | The VIX starts in 1990 and the sample starts in 1980. |
| Credit measure | Bank credit to the private sector (FD.AST.PRVT.GD.ZS) is the main credit series, not total domestic credit to the private sector (FS.AST.PRVT.GD.ZS). | Coverage in the sample is clearly better; for example the second series has no data for Thailand before 2007. |
| Government debt | Central government debt from the Global Debt Database, not WEO general government gross debt. | The WEO series covers only 9 percent of country-years in the 1980s; the GDD series covers 63 percent. |
| Missing data: variables | A candidate variable is dropped if it is missing for more than one third of the sample's country-years. | Same threshold as the IMF's fiscal crisis model in the Vulnerability Exercise. |
| Label: target | Target = 1 for country-year t if a systemic banking crisis starts in t+1 or t+2. | Same two-year horizon as the IMF's models; it leaves policymakers time to act. |
| Label: years during a crisis | Country-years from a crisis's start year to its end year are removed. The two episodes with no end year yet (Vietnam 2022, Sri Lanka 2023) are treated as ongoing. | During a crisis the indicators are distorted by the crisis itself; keeping those years labelled 'no crisis ahead' would teach the model that deep recessions are safe (post-crisis bias). |
| Predictor timing | Year-t values of the predictors are used; no extra lag. | Follows the IMF's models. Limitation: annual data for year t are published during t+1 and later revised, so this is not a strict real-time test. |
| Extreme values | Inflation and the exchange-rate change enter as log rates. In the logit model all predictors are also winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles, with cut-offs computed on the training window only. Tree models use the unwinsorised values. | Zimbabwe's 2008 inflation is 231 million percent. Trees only use the ordering of values, but a logit coefficient would be driven by a handful of hyperinflations. |
| Missing data: country-years | A country-year is dropped if more than half of its country-level predictors are missing. | Replaces a population cut-off: a row that is mostly imputed carries no information about that country. |
| Missing data: imputation | Remaining gaps are left empty in the model table and filled with the median inside each training window of the backtest. | Filling them here with a median computed over all years would leak information from the future into earlier training windows. |
| Backtest design | Expanding window: to predict from year T, train on years up to T-2. Test years 2001 to 2023. Hyperparameters are tuned once on the first training window (up to 1999) with time-block cross-validation and then kept fixed. | The target of a row from year T-1 is not known until T+1, so it cannot be used at T. Tuning on later data would let the test period influence the model. |
| Evaluation designs | Three are reported side by side: the real-time backtest, time-block cross-validation (5-year blocks held out, 2 years dropped on each side), and shuffled 5-fold cross-validation. The last one is labelled invalid and shown only as a reference. | The backtest is dominated by one event, the 2007-08 crisis. The IMF notes the same risk and complements its backtest with cross-validation on consecutive years. The shuffled version shows how much a naive split overstates performance. |
| Variable set 2 | A second variable set follows Table 3 of the IMF's financial sector model (TNM 21/03) wherever public data exist: 20 of its 31 variables. It was built after the first set had been backtested and uses the same country-years. | The first set was chosen from the general early-warning literature, not from the IMF's list. Reporting both, and saying which came second, keeps the comparison honest. |
| Variable set 2: sparse variables | Variables with many gaps (capital adequacy, bank Z-score, external debt) are kept and filled with the training-window median. | This is what the IMF's financial model does; the one-third rule used for set 1 comes from its fiscal model. |
| Variable set 2: not available | Bank, non-bank and corporate default probabilities, the sub-investment-grade share of corporate debt, house prices, the equity price gap, central bank liquidity support, the interest rate on new public debt and long-term bond yield growth are not included. | Commercial data, or public data with too few countries or years. |
| No further model changes | After the two variable sets gave the same answer, no more variables or models were tried. | Continuing to adjust the model until the test results improve would be selecting on the test data. |
| Explanations | Shapley values come from a random forest fitted on the whole sample. Fitted scores are shown next to out-of-sample scores wherever they appear. | This follows the IMF's presentation. It describes the patterns in the data; it is not evidence of forecasting ability. |

## Quality checks

- Rows loaded: dim_country 218, dim_series 98, fact_country_year 613,238, fact_global_year 549, crisis_episode 164. Primary and foreign keys were enforced by the database on insert.
- Sample: 163 countries x 1980-2023 = 7,172 country-years. Crises that can be predicted from it (starting 1981-2025): 158, of which 13 borderline.
- Stock ratios that cannot be negative (credit, money, debt, reserves, trade): no negative values.
- Inflation above 100 percent: 191 country-years. Largest: Zimbabwe 2008 (231,150,889%); Venezuela, RB 2018 (65,374%); Congo, Dem. Rep. 1994 (23,773%); Venezuela, RB 2019 (19,906%); Nicaragua 1987 (13,110%). These are real hyperinflations, not errors, but they will dominate a logit model unless the variable is winsorised or transformed.
- GDP growth outside -30 to +50 percent: 19 of 6,637 country-years (wars, oil discoveries, post-conflict rebounds).
- Agreement between sources, GDP growth in WDI and WEO: 6,540 common country-years, correlation 0.916, 8% differ by more than 2 percentage points (different vintages and base years).
